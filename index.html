Refinancing Central Coast Buyer Tips Explained

The first step in any refinancing decision is understanding exactly what you are paying now. If your home loan is more than two years old, there is a high probability your current rate is not competitive. A useful rule of thumb is that a 0.5 percentage point difference on a $600,000 balance saves roughly $3,000 per year in interest. On a $500,000 balance, that saving is around $2,500. These savings typically cover standard discharge and application fees within the first year.

However, the calculation only works if you factor in the total cost to switch. This includes discharge fees from your existing lender, new loan application fees, and government registration charges. A broker can model these figures for you. For a detailed breakdown of when switching makes financial sense, see the complete guide to refinancing central coast.

Leverage Local Equity Growth

Property values on the Central Coast have grown substantially since 2020. For homeowners in suburbs like Gosford, Wyong and Terrigal, this growth often translates to increased equity. A lower loan-to-value ratio (LVR) is a key lever for securing better rates. Lenders typically price their loans more aggressively for borrowers with an LVR below 80 per cent, and even better for those below 60 per cent.

If your equity has increased, you may also be in a position to access funds for renovations or investment without paying Lenders Mortgage Insurance. When planning a move, considering refinancing central coast options can reveal if you have enough equity to consolidate debt or upgrade your home, potentially at a lower interest rate than personal loans or credit cards.

Evaluate Incentives Carefully

Many lenders advertise cashback offers of $2,000 to $4,000 to entice borrowers to switch. While these offers provide an immediate cash benefit, they can sometimes distract from a higher ongoing interest rate. The most reliable way to assess value is to compare the total interest payable over three years, including the cashback, against a loan with a lower rate but no cashback.

In many cases, the lower-rate loan delivers superior savings after the first 18 months. A broker will present this comparison in writing, showing you the long-term cost rather than just the upfront headline. This ensures you choose a loan that saves money over the term, not just at settlement.

Prepare for a New Borrower Assessment

When you refinance, lenders assess you as if you are a new customer. This means they will scrutinise your income, employment stability, living expenses and credit file. Even if you have been a perfect customer with your current lender, you must meet the new lender's serviceability requirements.

Self-employed borrowers or those with irregular income need to be particularly prepared. Having your tax returns, business activity statements (BAS) and accountant declarations ready can streamline the process. A broker can direct you to specialist lenders who understand complex income structures, ensuring your application is presented in the best possible light.

  1. Review your current loan. Check your current interest rate, remaining term and fees. Determine if your rate is above the market average for your LVR.
  2. Request a payout figure. Contact your existing lender to ask for a discharge payout figure. This will list any break costs if you are on a fixed rate.
  3. Estimate your property value. Get a realistic idea of your current property value to calculate your equity. This helps predict the LVR and rates you can access.
  4. Compare loans. Use a broker to compare products from a panel of lenders. Look at the effective rate (rate plus fees) rather than just the headline rate.
  5. Apply and settle. Submit your application to the chosen lender. Once approved, your new loan will settle and your old mortgage will be discharged.

Common questions

How do I calculate if refinancing is worth it? Compare your annual interest savings against the total cost to switch. For example, a 0.5 per cent rate reduction on a $500,000 loan saves about $2,500 a year. If switching costs are around $1,500, you break even in roughly seven months and save money thereafter.

Can I refinance if I am self-employed? Yes, but you may need to provide alternative documentation such as BAS or accountant declarations. Specialist lenders within a broker's panel often have policies tailored for self-employed borrowers.

What happens to my offset account when I refinance? If your new loan has an offset feature, the funds can be transferred. However, if the new loan does not offer an offset, those funds will no longer reduce your interest, potentially affecting your overall repayment strategy.
